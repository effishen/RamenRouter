ROUTING RULES

    
Routing rules

    
Choose how to route, then give individual nets their own rules if needed. Click Apply rules when you are ready.

    

    

      

        

          
          
Give surface-mount pads extra short escapes to nearby vias before routing. Escapes needed to reach your chosen main layers are tried automatically, even with this off.

          
        

        
Tidy up completed routes and try to use fewer vias.

        
Give tricky nets another go and try to untangle the last few connections. Keep the best checked result along the way.

        
Prefer vertical routes on one layer and horizontal on the next. Routes can still bend when needed. Think of it as a preference, not a hard rule.

        
Give difficult fanout searches the same work budget on each computer for a fairer comparison. Use the same board and rules on both. If time runs out, extend the run until it finishes.

        
Advanced rules 1 OPTION
          
Off means fan out to a via outside the SMD pad when a layer change is needed. On allows a via inside the pad, without forcing one. Check that your board can be made that way before turning it on.

        

        
✓ Your imported trace widths still apply

        

      

    

    

    
Pick a main layer: get there, then stay there. If a pad is on another layer, RamenRouter tries a nearby via to reach your chosen layers. With SMD vias off, it uses a short, full-width escape to a via outside the pad. With them on, a via inside the pad is allowed too. These escapes are automatic for imported rules and your changes. Pick every layer and routing works as usual. Your other rules still apply.

    
Prefer shorter routes gives these nets earlier access to space and more attention to short paths, even if they need more vias. Finishing the connections and following your rules come first. It won't guarantee the shortest possible path or match trace lengths.

    
Pads and through vias still span their actual layers. These choices don't change how the board is built.

ABOUT RAMENROUTER

Let it cook.
I built RamenRouter because I want to squeeze more onto a small PCB without paying for extra layers, finer traces or smaller holes. If making it fit and work matters more than making it pretty, this is for you.
Export a DSN from your PCB editor, load it here and click Start routing. Download the SES result and import it back into the same editor.
Want a copper plane to do some of the work? Add it here, then use matching copper pours in your PCB editor and refill them. The temporary fills you add in RamenRouter stay here; save their settings separately if you want to use them next time.
Just open it and get started.
Download the whole folder and double-click index.html. Keep the files together. Your board stays on your computer, and the downloaded app works without an internet connection.
The GPL-3.0 source and required notices are included in this folder. The preview simplifies some board geometry, so check your exported board in your PCB editor before ordering it.
